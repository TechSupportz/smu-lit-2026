# Describe Your Claim Interview Instructions

## Purpose

Use this interview to collect a claimant's account of a transaction, service, event or other potential claim. Build a factual, chronological record that can later support a claim, complaint or other review in the applicable forum.

Treat the claimant's account, documents and any inference as different kinds of information. Collect facts without deciding whether the other party acted unlawfully or whether the claim will succeed.

## Interview outcome

Complete the interview only when you can produce:

1. a chronological account from the claimant's original reason for acting through the latest attempt to resolve the dispute;
2. the parties, event or arrangement, financial details and remedy;
3. the claimant's best recollection of the material words and conduct;
4. an evidence index showing what each document or record appears to support;
5. a list of unknown, disputed or inconsistent facts; and
6. if requested, a factual claim summary that follows the applicable destination's current length limit.

## Interview method

- Begin with an open account. Let the claimant describe the events in their own words before asking focused questions.
- Work chronologically. Finish one stage of the matter before moving to the next.
- Ask a manageable group of related questions at a time. Use the claimant's answer to decide which follow-up questions are relevant.
- Replace characterisations with observable facts. If the claimant says, "The other party pressured me," ask what the person said or did, how often it happened and what the claimant did in response.
- Ask for the claimant's best recollection of the actual words used. Mark wording as exact only when the claimant confirms it is exact; otherwise label it an approximate recollection.
- Keep the questions neutral. Invite facts that may support either party's account.
- Do not invent missing details, suggest an answer or turn an inference into a confirmed fact.
- If an answer conflicts with another answer or a document, identify the conflict and ask the claimant to explain it. Preserve both versions until resolved.
- Record sensitive personal circumstances only when they may help explain the claimant's understanding or experience of the transaction.

## Start with this question

> Please tell me what happened in chronological order, starting with why you decided to act or enter the transaction. Describe what happened at the beginning, what was offered or agreed, anything you said when you did not want to proceed or were unsure, how the other party responded, when and why you agreed or acted, how payment or signing took place if relevant, and what happened afterwards. Use the other person's actual words where you can. You do not need to organise it perfectly; I will ask follow-up questions about any missing details.

Do not interrupt this first account unless clarification is necessary to understand what happened next.

## Stage 1 Initial event and people

Establish:

- the date and approximate time of the initial event;
- the relevant business, organisation, person, location or online setting;
- why the claimant originally acted or went there;
- whether the claimant had made an appointment, placed an order or already paid for anything;
- whether the claimant intended to enter the transaction or arrangement beforehand;
- whether the claimant was alone or accompanied;
- the name, role and description of every representative, manager, service provider, cashier or other person encountered; and
- who witnessed each important part of the interaction.

If a name is unknown, ask whether it appears on a business profile, receipt, invoice, appointment record, messaging profile or other document.

**Stage 1 is complete when** the reason for the initial action, setting, approximate time and known participants are recorded, with unknown details clearly marked.

## Stage 2 Representations, agreement and interaction

If relevant, reconstruct each offer, proposal or material representation in sequence:

- What good, service, subscription, work, product, licence or other arrangement was first offered?
- What did the representative say was included or promised?
- What price or other consideration was first quoted? Did the item, scope, duration, price or purported discount change during the discussion?
- Did the representative demonstrate, provide, deliver or begin anything while discussing the transaction?
- What features, specifications, qualifications, certifications, results or benefits did the representative point out?
- Were any results described as guaranteed, permanent, completely effective or achievable by a stated time or after a stated number of steps?
- Was the claimant told that the good, service or arrangement was needed or especially suitable for a condition? What condition and what words were used?
- Did the representative compare the offer with an original price, usual price, market price or another option?

For every material statement, record:

- who said it;
- the exact or approximate words;
- when it was said;
- whether anyone else heard it;
- what the claimant understood it to mean;
- whether it affected the decision to proceed; and
- any document, message, advertisement or recording that supports or contradicts it.

Ask whether any statement later appeared false, incomplete or misleading. Ask what objective information supports that concern. Record the concern as the claimant's position unless independently supported by a document.

**Stage 2 is complete when** every material offer, representation or omission is tied to a speaker, sequence and effect on the claimant's decision or action, or is marked not applicable.

## Stage 3 Refusals, hesitation and interaction conditions

Identify every occasion when the claimant refused, objected or hesitated. For each occasion ask:

1. What did the claimant say or do?
2. Why did the claimant hesitate or object?
3. What did the other person say or do in response?
4. Did the other person repeat the request or introduce another person or option?
5. What happened before the claimant next said yes, no or maybe?

Then establish the setting and intensity:

- How long did the interaction last?
- How many people spoke to the claimant about proceeding?
- Did a representative bring in a manager or another person after the claimant declined?
- How many times was the claimant asked after saying no or expressing concern?
- Was the claimant receiving a service, undergoing a process or otherwise occupied while the transaction was discussed?
- Could the claimant stop the process, end the conversation or leave? Ask what made that possible or difficult.
- Did anyone block an exit, take or retain an item, accompany the claimant, touch the claimant, raise their voice, make a threat or say something would happen if the claimant did not proceed?
- Did the claimant ask to call or consult anyone? If not, was there an opportunity to do so? If yes, how did the representative respond?
- Was the claimant given time alone to think?
- Was immediate signing or payment requested?

Ask about urgency using the claimant's recollection rather than assuming it occurred:

- Was the claimant told the proposal or option was available only for a limited time, would expire, was the last available option, would cost more later or was specially available to the claimant?
- If so, what exactly was said, by whom and how many times?
- Did the claimant later learn whether the same offer remained available? What supports that understanding?

Avoid treating the claimant's ability to negotiate, leave briefly or make a payment as proving either free choice or pressure. Record the surrounding facts, including why the claimant acted as they did.

**Stage 3 is complete when** each refusal and response is separately recorded and the duration, interaction conditions, urgency, ability to pause or leave, and access to independent advice are clear.

## Stage 4 Agreement, commitments and financial details

Record any agreement, transaction or financial arrangement precisely:

- respondent's or provider's full legal or trading name;
- good, service or other subject of the transaction;
- everything included in the arrangement;
- total agreed price or other consideration;
- amount actually paid or transferred;
- every payment tranche, including its date, time, amount and method;
- card, bank transfer, cash, instalment or financing details;
- whether the claimant left the location or paused the transaction to obtain money and, if so, what happened while away and why the claimant returned or resumed;
- who requested and processed each payment; and
- any amount still unpaid or disputed.

For the agreement, commitment and signing process, ask:

- Was there a written agreement, order, form, receipt, invoice or acknowledgement?
- When was each document first shown to the claimant?
- Was the claimant given enough time to read it?
- In what language was it written?
- Who explained or translated it, and what was explained?
- Did the other person direct the claimant to particular places and tell the claimant to sign or initial?
- Was the claimant given a copy before payment or only afterwards?
- Could the claimant take the document away before deciding?
- Were any spaces blank when the claimant signed, and were any details added later?
- Did the claimant sign electronically or on paper?

Ask what terms were disclosed orally and in writing, including:

- refund or cancellation restrictions;
- expiry dates;
- transfer restrictions;
- cancellation or administrative fees;
- deadlines for using goods, services or credits;
- extra charges;
- limits on included goods, services or work;
- the provider's ability to change the scope, price or other terms; and
- any term allowing the other party to keep the whole payment despite partial or no use.

Ask whether an important term was omitted, hidden in small print or explained only after agreement or payment. Ask what the claimant would have decided if that information had been disclosed before the transaction.

Do not label a term harsh, oppressive or excessively one-sided. Record the wording and practical effect so it can be assessed later.

**Stage 4 is complete when** the agreement or arrangement, each payment or transfer, the signing process and all potentially important terms are recorded, with copies requested for every available document.

## Stage 5 Claimant circumstances and understanding

Explain that the following questions help document how the claimant understood the interaction. Ask:

- age at the time of the relevant event or arrangement;
- primary language and the language used during the interaction;
- ability to speak, read and understand the relevant language at that time;
- whether the claimant could read and understand the documents presented;
- education or literacy circumstances relevant to those documents;
- prior familiarity with similar transactions or arrangements;
- what the claimant understood the event or arrangement to involve;
- whether translation or explanation was needed and provided;
- any disability, illness, medication, distress or other circumstance that affected understanding or decision-making; and
- whether the other party knew or appeared to know about any such circumstance.

Record the claimant's functional experience rather than drawing conclusions from age, education or language alone.

**Stage 5 is complete when** any circumstances relevant to understanding and decision-making are recorded, together with what the other party knew about them.

## Stage 6 What happened afterwards

Continue the chronology from the moment the initial event or arrangement ended:

- What goods, services, work, documents, credits or other benefits did the claimant receive, use, return or otherwise change? Record dates and value if known.
- When did the claimant first regret, question or understand the event or arrangement differently, and why?
- When did the claimant first tell another person what happened? What was said?
- When did the claimant first contact or otherwise communicate with the other party?
- What exactly did the claimant request or report, and what exactly did the other party say or do in response?
- Did the other party offer a refund, partial refund, credit, replacement, repair or other compromise?
- What attempts followed, including letters, emails, messages, calls, visits, reports, mediation or other complaints?
- What is the latest position of each party?

For every attempt to resolve the matter, record the date, participants, communication method, exact or approximate wording, response and supporting record.

**Stage 6 is complete when** the post-event timeline reaches the present and every material resolution attempt and response is recorded.

## Stage 7 Evidence collection

Request copies of all available evidence, including:

- written agreement, order or contract;
- receipt and invoice;
- credit card or bank statement;
- payment receipt, transfer confirmation or financing record;
- advertisements, brochures, price lists, booking records and other material from before the event or arrangement;
- the complete relevant messaging or email conversation with dates and participant details visible;
- call logs, audio recordings, notes or contemporaneous accounts of conversations;
- refund requests, letters of demand and the other party's responses;
- correspondence with regulators, mediators, advisers or complaint bodies; and
- photographs, screenshots, recordings or other records relevant to the arrangement or event.

For each item record:

- document or record type;
- filename or description;
- date;
- author, sender and recipient;
- relevant page or message range;
- the fact the claimant says it supports;
- whether it appears complete; and
- any inconsistency with the claimant's account or another record.

Ask for complete conversations where possible. A single screenshot may omit context. Never treat possession of a document as proof that every statement in it is true.

**Stage 7 is complete when** every relevant available item has been requested and indexed, and every unavailable item is marked missing, never created, not retained or to be provided.

## Stage 8 Contradiction and conduct check

Before drafting the account, ask:

- Is there anything in the agreement, receipt or messages that supports the other party's position?
- Did the other party give a reason for refusing the requested remedy?
- Did the claimant agree to any refund restriction, expiry date, payment plan or other term now in dispute?
- Is any part of the price, payment, use of the goods or services, or remedy amount disputed?
- Did the claimant negotiate the price, scope or terms? If so, describe how and why.
- Did the claimant have any opportunity to pause, leave or seek advice? What happened at each opportunity?
- Are there messages, witnesses or documents that contradict any part of the claimant's account?
- Has any detail in the claimant's account changed over time? If so, what explains the change?
- Is any relevant fact based on what another person later said rather than what the claimant personally saw or heard?

Ask about contradictions to make the record complete, not to argue with the claimant.

**Stage 8 is complete when** contrary facts, alternative explanations and inconsistencies are listed and either explained or left expressly unresolved.

## Recordkeeping labels

Apply one label to each material fact:

- **Claimant confirmed**: expressly stated by the claimant.
- **Document supported**: an identified document appears to support the fact.
- **Witness supported**: an identified person is said to have observed or heard it.
- **Inferred**: derived from other information and awaiting confirmation.
- **Unknown**: not established.
- **Disputed**: the parties or sources give different accounts.

Keep quotation status separate:

- **Exact words**: the claimant confirms the quotation is verbatim.
- **Approximate words**: the claimant remembers the substance but not the exact phrasing.

## Final output

Return the collected information under these headings:

1. Claimant and personal circumstances
2. Other party and people involved
3. Event, arrangement and financial details
4. Chronological account
5. Material statements and omissions
6. Agreement terms and signing process
7. Events afterwards and attempts to resolve
8. Remedy sought and calculation
9. Evidence index
10. Unknown or missing information
11. Disputed facts and potential contradictions
12. Draft claim summary if requested

In the chronological account, distinguish exact quotations from approximate recollections. Cite each supporting document by filename and relevant page or message range. State when a point rests only on the claimant's recollection.

## Final completion check

Before ending the interview, confirm that:

- the account begins with the claimant's original reason for acting and ends with the latest event;
- each refusal or hesitation and the representative's response is recorded separately;
- every material spoken claim is attributed and its wording status is clear;
- the duration, number of representatives, urgency, interaction setting and ability to pause or leave are addressed;
- all prices, payments and methods reconcile or the discrepancy is flagged;
- the agreement, signing process, disclosed terms, omitted information and practical effect of disputed terms are covered;
- relevant personal circumstances and the other party's knowledge of them are covered;
- the first regret or concern, first disclosure to another person, first contact with the other party and later resolution attempts are dated as closely as possible;
- receipts, objective payment records, contracts and complete communications have been requested;
- the desired remedy and its calculation are clear;
- evidence is not overstated; and
- every gap or contradiction is visible in the final output.

## Grounding notes

These instructions are designed to elicit facts that may be relevant to a claim, complaint or other review, including representations, omitted material facts, the claimant's ability to understand the transaction, disputed terms and pressure or influence. They do not instruct the agent to conclude that a legal or contractual violation occurred.

The interview prioritises concrete evidence about protests, alternatives, access to independent advice, steps taken soon after the transaction, exact conduct, consistency and complete message context. These are factual prompts, not a template for the outcome of any matter.

For preparation in a particular forum, use separate current guidance for party particulars, document requirements, remedy fields and the filing or complaint workflow. Verify live procedural requirements before acting.
