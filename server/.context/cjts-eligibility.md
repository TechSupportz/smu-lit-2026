Before filing, check that:
- The claim falls within the SCT’s jurisdiction (for example, certain disputes involving goods, services, or residential tenancy agreements).
- The claim is generally not more than $20,000, or up to $30,000 if both parties have signed a Memorandum of Consent.
- The claim is filed within 2 years of the event giving rise to the claim.
- The respondent is located in Singapore.
- The dispute is not one of the matters excluded from SCT jurisdiction.
You should first complete the pre-filing assessment on the Community Justice and Tribunals System (CJTS). This gives you a pre-filing assessment ID.
You will generally need:
- Your particulars;
- The respondent's particulars and address;
- A summary of what you are claiming;
- Supporting documents such as contracts, invoices, receipts, photographs, emails, messages, tenancy agreements, etc.
The documents should generally be uploaded as PDFs. If the respondent is a business, you will also need its ACRA business profile obtained within 1 month of filing.
Log in to CJTS and go to:
Online Applications → Claim Form
You enter the claimant and respondent details, describe the claim, state the amount claimed, upload your supporting documents, and pay the filing fee.

Contents of claim
16.  A claim must contain —
(a)	the name and address of each claimant and, in the case of a representative claim, the name and address of each person represented;
(b)	the name and address of each respondent;
(c)	the sum of money claimed by each claimant or person represented; and
(d)	such other particulars of the claim as are reasonably sufficient to inform the respondent of the ground for the claim and the manner in which the amount claimed by each claimant or person represented has been calculated.
After payment, you select a consultation date and time and obtain the Claim Form and Notice of Consultation.
This is an important step.
You must serve the Claim Form and Notice of Consultation on the respondent within 7 days after filing the claim.
Service can generally be done by:
- Personal delivery; or
- Registered post.
For a company, for example, the documents can be delivered to its staff or sent by registered post to its ACRA-registered address.
After serving the respondent, you must go back into CJTS and file a Declaration of Service (DOS) before the first consultation.
You should upload proof of service, such as the registered-post receipt or an acknowledgement signed by the respondent.
So, procedurally:
Pre-filing assessment → Claim Form → Pay fee → Consultation date → Serve respondent → Declaration of Service
Both parties are required to attend the consultation.
A Registrar will try to help the parties resolve the dispute. You can also use CJTS's eNegotiation or eMediation facilities to try to settle the matter before attending court.
If the matter settles, the proceedings can conclude there.
If the dispute cannot be resolved at the consultation, it may proceed to a hearing before a Tribunal Magistrate.
You should be prepared to explain:
- What happened;
- Why you say the respondent is liable;
- How you calculated the amount claimed; and
- What evidence supports each part of your claim.
The SCT specifically advises parties to be able to explain each component of their claim and to submit all relevant evidence.
After considering the parties' positions and evidence, the Tribunal Magistrate may make an order determining the claim.
If the successful party obtains an order and the other party does not comply, there are procedures available to enforce the SCT order.
One important point: lawyers are generally not permitted to represent parties in SCT proceedings.
