# CJTS small-claim form preparation checklist

Source basis: `cjts_guide_filing_small_claims_online.pdf`, dated 14 April 2022. Page references below use the page numbers printed in that guide. This is a source-faithful preparation list, not confirmation that the present CJTS interface or legal requirements are unchanged.

## Before opening the claim form

- [ ] Decide the single main **nature of dispute** and the applicable **sub-category/type of dispute**. The guide shows these main categories: contract for sale of goods, contract for provision of services, damage to property, and lease not exceeding two years (residential premises). Claims against the same party spanning more than one main category must be filed separately. (pp. 5-6)
- [ ] Know the **date the cause of action arose**, in `DD/MM/YYYY` format. (p. 6)
- [ ] Know the **total claim amount** in SGD. (p. 6)
- [ ] Be ready to answer adaptive pre-filing questions about **party details**, **nature of dispute**, and **service of documents**. The exact questions depend on earlier answers and the dispute type. (pp. 6-7)
- [ ] Complete the pre-filing assessment and record the resulting **Pre-filing Reference ID**. It is required for the claim form, is not displayed on the CJTS home screen, and is valid for seven days. (pp. 8, 24)
- [ ] Have access to the relevant login method: **Singpass** for an individual filing personally, **Corppass** for an entity, or **CJTS Pass** if Singpass is unavailable. (pp. 9, 24)
- [ ] Set aside approximately **15 minutes** to complete the claim form. A saved draft expires after seven days. (p. 24, screenshot instructions)
- [ ] At the Terms and Conditions screen, read the complete terms, tick the agreement box, and enter the CAPTCHA. (p. 4)
- [ ] Avoid the browser's Back, Forward, and Refresh controls while submitting; the guide warns that these may disrupt the operation. (p. 4)

## Pre-filing questions shown in the guide's rental-deposit example

These are example-specific and should not be treated as the universal question set.

### Parties

- [ ] Are you claiming as an individual?
- [ ] Are you a bankrupt?
- [ ] Are you claiming against the correct party with whom you have a contractual obligation?
- [ ] Is the other party an individual?
- [ ] Is the other party a bankrupt?
- [ ] Is there a mediation or arbitration clause in your agreement?

### Nature of the rental dispute

- [ ] Is the tenancy agreement for residential premises?
- [ ] Did the cause of action arise on or before the date shown by CJTS?
- [ ] Does the tenancy meet the minimum period asked about by CJTS?
- [ ] Has stamp duty been paid on the tenancy agreement?
- [ ] Do you have a breakdown of individual items and amounts supporting the total claimed?
- [ ] Do you have documents supporting the amount claimed?
- [ ] Can you show the condition of the premises at the start and end of the tenancy?
- [ ] Are you seeking a money order, work order, and/or vacant-possession order?

### Service

- [ ] Does the opposing party reside in Singapore, have a registered address in Singapore, or - if a company - operate or have a registered address here?
- [ ] Can you locate the opposing party and personally serve or otherwise bring the claim to that party's attention in Singapore?

(All questions in this section are transcribed or closely restated from the example on p. 7.)

## A. Particulars of claimant(s)

CJTS retrieves claimant details from the applicant's profile and the pre-filing assessment, but the guide says they can be amended before submission. (p. 25)

- [ ] Full name.
- [ ] Identification type and number, such as NRIC, FIN, or passport number.
- [ ] Contact number 1, including phone type and country code. The Tribunals may use this number to communicate with you.
- [ ] Contact number 2, if any.
- [ ] Valid email address. The Tribunals may use it for electronic mail.
- [ ] Registered/service address:
    - [ ] premises type;
    - [ ] postal code;
    - [ ] block or house number;
    - [ ] street name;
    - [ ] floor and unit number, where applicable;
    - [ ] building name, where applicable; and
    - [ ] country.
- [ ] Details of every additional claimant, if any.
- [ ] Every additional address for service, if any.

Important: the guide says additional claimants, respondents, and addresses cannot subsequently be removed after the claim is submitted. (p. 25)

## B. Particulars of respondent(s)

- [ ] Exact full name or registered entity name.
- [ ] Identification type and number, such as NRIC, FIN, UEN, or passport number.
- [ ] Contact number 1, including phone type and country code, if known.
- [ ] Contact number 2, if known.
- [ ] Email address, if known.
- [ ] Registered/service address:
    - [ ] premises type;
    - [ ] postal code;
    - [ ] block or house number;
    - [ ] street name;
    - [ ] floor and unit number, where applicable;
    - [ ] building name, where applicable; and
    - [ ] country.
- [ ] Details of every additional respondent, if any.
- [ ] Every additional address for service, if any.

The guide states that every field marked with an asterisk in CJTS is mandatory. (p. 26)

## C. Particulars of claim

- [ ] Confirm the **nature of dispute** and **type of dispute** retrieved from pre-filing.
- [ ] Enter the **name/type of goods sold or services provided** where CJTS asks for it; the guide describes this as mandatory.
- [ ] Prepare all other factual fields that appear for the selected dispute type. The guide says these vary with the nature of dispute and that fuller information assists processing. (p. 26)

### Rental-deposit example only

The guide's screenshot for a residential lease refund shows these fields; other dispute types will present different fields. (p. 26)

- [ ] Location of rental premises.
- [ ] Deposit paid in SGD.
- [ ] Monthly rent in SGD.
- [ ] Date of tenancy agreement.
- [ ] Tenancy start date stated in the agreement.
- [ ] Tenancy expiry date stated in the agreement.

## D. Brief summary of claim

- [ ] Prepare a factual summary of no more than **500 characters**. (p. 27)
- [ ] Include, as space permits: what was agreed, what happened, when it happened, what remains unresolved, and the remedy sought.

## E. Supporting documents

- [ ] Convert each attachment to **PDF**.
- [ ] Keep each PDF at or below **5 MB**.
- [ ] Remove special characters from filenames; the screenshot specifically gives `@`, `/`, `\`, and `%` as examples that filenames cannot contain.
- [ ] For every attachment, prepare:
    - [ ] the CJTS document type;
    - [ ] a short document description; and
    - [ ] the page number being referred to.
- [ ] Check the document before uploading: the guide says a submitted document cannot be deleted or removed from the system.
- [ ] If either claimant or respondent is not an individual, obtain and upload the **latest ACRA Business Profile** for that party.
- [ ] Gather evidence relevant to the dispute, such as an agreement, receipt, invoice, correspondence, photographs, itemised loss calculation, or proof of payment. CJTS suggests document types according to the dispute. The guide's rental example shows a rental agreement and stamp-duty document. (pp. 24, 27)

Privacy warning: the claim-form instructions say all entered details and uploaded documents will be visible to the respondent, except the identification number. Review attachments for unrelated sensitive information before submitting them. (p. 24, screenshot instructions)

## F. Remedy - "Claiming for"

More than one remedy type may be selected. (pp. 27-28)

- [ ] **Money order:** exact value claimed in SGD.
- [ ] **Work order:** each work item sought and the substitute/alternative monetary value for that work.
- [ ] Additional work orders, if more than one item is sought.
- [ ] **Costs**, if requested, with supporting evidence.
- [ ] **Disbursements**, if requested, with supporting evidence.

The guide states that costs and disbursements are awarded at the Tribunals' discretion. (p. 28)

The rental-deposit pre-filing example also asks whether the claimant seeks an order for vacant possession. That option is not shown as a universal remedy in the guide's claim-form screenshot; rely on the dispute-specific live form. (pp. 7, 27)

## Review, declaration, payment, and appointment choices

- [ ] Review all information on the confirmation page.
- [ ] Be ready to make the declaration that you are the claimant and that the information provided is true and correct. (p. 30)
- [ ] Choose a payment method: internet banking, credit card, or Pay Later/offline payment. The guide warns there is no refund of fees for an incorrect claim. (pp. 24, 31)
- [ ] If using Pay Later, generate the Payment Advice and pay within the guide's stated three-day draft window through CJTS or at the State Courts Finance Counter/ACS machine. (p. 34)
- [ ] State whether you understand and speak English. If not, select the language you understand and speak. The guide shows Cantonese, Hokkien, Malay, Mandarin, Tamil, Teochew, and Others. If "Others" is selected, the guide says the user must arrange a qualified interpreter, subject to Registry approval. (p. 32)
- [ ] Select an available consultation date and time. (p. 32)

The date-selection screen warns that, unless otherwise directed, an unresolved case may be fixed for hearing on the same day as the consultation or the next working day. (pp. 31-32, screenshot instructions)

## Records to save after filing

- [ ] Claim/case number.
- [ ] First consultation date and time.
- [ ] Payment receipt PDF.
- [ ] Claimant copy: notice of consultation and claim form.
- [ ] Respondent copy: notice, claim details, barcode, and one-time reference number needed by the respondent to access the case. (p. 33)
- [ ] Serve the correct Respondent Copy on every respondent. The guide warns that the SCT may be unable to proceed if service cannot be completed; each respondent has a unique one-time reference number. (p. 33, acknowledgement screenshot)

## Source limitations

- The source guide was created in April 2022 and presents a historical CJTS interface.
- The guide does not give one universal list of dispute-specific claim-detail fields; those fields change with the selected nature and type of dispute.
- The guide's worked pre-filing and claim-detail example concerns a residential tenancy deposit refund. Those example-only questions and fields are labelled above.
- This checklist does not add current fee amounts, jurisdictional limits, limitation periods, or legal conclusions that are not stated in the supplied guide.
